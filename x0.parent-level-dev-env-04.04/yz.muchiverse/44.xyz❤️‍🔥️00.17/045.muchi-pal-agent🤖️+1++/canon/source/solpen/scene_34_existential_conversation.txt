INT. MARTIAN COURIER SHIP - COMMON AREA

SOUND of low music playing from a portable speaker, the clink of vials, the loose laughter of people who've survived too much

The common area of the transport ship has been transformed into a makeshift lounge. Lucky, Astra, Gem (recovered), LAUREN (a female clone soldier with sharp features), and three other FEMALE CLONES are sprawled across the floor, taking a break from the monotonous production of bug spray—their only defense against the Carcazods.

LUCKY, his platinum blonde high-top fade dyed a mellow lavender for the occasion, lies on his back. ASTRA, her asymmetrical hair full of beads and her plushie keychains—a purple monster and a small green alien—spread out around her, sits cross-legged.

They're high. A combination of Type C blood derivatives and Astra's genetically optimized cannabis from the ranch.

<center>LUCKY</center>
> (staring at the ceiling)
> Do you think God has a SolPen?

Silence. Then laughter.

<center>ASTRA</center>
> (eyes red)
> What?

<center>LUCKY</center>
> Think about it. If God created the universe, what did He use? Some kind of... cosmic pen. Some tool that turns thought into reality. That's literally what the SolPen does.

<center>LAUREN</center>
> (propped on one elbow)
> So God is... Sol?

<center>GEM</center>
> (smiling)
> Or Sol is God. From the future.

<center>ASTRA</center>
> That's the most ridiculous thing I've ever heard.

<center>LUCKY</center>
> Is it? Sol can create matter from thought. He can rewrite reality. He built a civilization inside the Moon. If that's not godlike, what is?

<center>LAUREN</center>
> Maybe the SolPen IS the divine tool. Maybe Sol didn't invent it. Maybe he found it.

The clones exchange glances. They were created. They know what it feels like to be someone else's thought made real.

<center>CLONE 2</center>
> (quietly)
> If Sol is God, then what are we? Angels? Or just... clay?

<center>ASTRA</center>
> You're individuals. You're not clay.

<center>CLONE 3</center>
> Are we? We were grown in tubes. Our souls are fuel for a weapon. That sounds like clay to me.

Lucky sits up, his muscular mixed-heritage frame casting a long shadow.

<center>LUCKY</center>
> I built an empire of info. Sol built an empire of clones. Neither of us asked if the people in our empires wanted to be there. That's the real question. Not whether Sol is God. Whether any of us have the right to play him.

Silence. The music plays — something slow, something from Earth that none of them will take for granted again.

<center>GEM</center>
> (softly)
> Is it the end of the world?

<center>LUCKY</center>
> No. It's the end of ONE world. The one we knew. New ones begin. That's the cycle.

<center>ASTRA</center>
> (leaning against him, her plushies clinking)
> You're very philosophical for someone who's currently high on bug spray.

<center>LUCKY</center>
> Bug spray is just DMT with a marketing problem.

They laugh. The kind of laugh that comes from exhaustion and the absurdity of existence. Lucky looks around at his team. His family.

FADE OUT.
