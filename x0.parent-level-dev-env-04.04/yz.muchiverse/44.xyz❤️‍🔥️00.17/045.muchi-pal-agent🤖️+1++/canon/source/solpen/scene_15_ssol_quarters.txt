INT. SSOL - RECREATION/LIVING QUARTERS - DAY

SOUND of low chatter, the clink of containers, a strange mix of military barracks and underground lounge

The recreation quarters of the SSOL are a sprawling, multi-level space carved into the ship's hull. Clone soldiers lounge on modular seating, some playing games with holographic pieces, others simply resting.

LUCKY enters, his platinum blonde high-top fade standing sharp against the amber lighting. He wears a red silk vest over his Exo Suit's chest plate, gold chains glinting. He carries himself with the swagger of a tech mogul who just realized he owns the room.

ASTRA follows, her asymmetrical hair (right side long and dark wavy, left buzzed with braids) bouncing as she moves. Plushie keychains of cute aliens and monsters jingle from her belt and bag. She holds a metallic vial glowing faintly red.

<center>ASTRA</center>
> Lucky. You need to see this. I found the engineering bypass.

She pulls up a holographic schematic.

<center>ASTRA</center>
> The ship isn't powered by fluctuations. It's fueled by clone deaths. There's a massive, half-heart, half-brain cyborg machine in the sub-levels. It processes biological matter into quantum energy.

Lucky stares at the schematic, his dark expressive eyes widening.

<center>LUCKY</center>
> (quietly)
> Sol built a life-sustained slaughterhouse.

<center>ASTRA</center>
> It's brutal. But effective. This whole society is a closed loop of creation and sacrifice.

A CLONE SOLDIER approaches, carrying a tray of identical vials.

<center>CLONE SOLDIER</center>
> Daily ration, sir. You'll feel the difference within minutes.

Lucky takes the vial, eyeing it suspiciously.

<center>LUCKY</center>
> I'm supposed to drink this?

<center>CLONE SOLDIER</center>
> Everyone does, sir. On Mars, it's how you breathe outside. Your stomach absorbs the oxygen directly. PFC-based synthetic blood. One vial is a day's life.

Lucky cracks the seal. The smell is coppery, sweet. He drinks. The rush is immediate—clarity, energy, his caramel skin flushing as oxygen saturates his system.

<center>LUCKY</center>
> (exhaling)
> Okay. That's... something else.

He looks at Astra. She's already sipped hers and is looking more relaxed.

<center>ASTRA</center>
> We should have some fun, Lucky. If we're going to fight a war, we need to live a little first.

She points to a group of clones dancing to a holographic light-show. Lucky grins, his sharp jawline set in a confident smile.

<center>LUCKY</center>
> You're right. Let's show these clones how a billionaire and a genius party.

They join the clones, Lucky's athletic physique moving with natural grace as he dances, for a moment forgetting the cyborg horror beneath their feet.

As they laugh, Lucky's eyes drift to a dark corner.

Something moves in those shadows.

Transparent. Shifting. Shimmering reptilian scales reflecting the holographic lights for a fraction of a second before vanishing into a seam in the wall.

Lucky stops. The cold ozone scent lingers.

<center>LUCKY</center>
> (quietly, to himself)
> Something's living on this ship that nobody's talking about.

He looks at Astra, who is laughing with a clone technician. He decides to let it go for now. He adjusts his platinum high-top fade and dives back into the fun.

FADE OUT.
