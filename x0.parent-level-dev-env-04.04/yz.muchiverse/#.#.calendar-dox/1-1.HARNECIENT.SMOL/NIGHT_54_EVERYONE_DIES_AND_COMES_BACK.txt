**THE NARRATOR:** NIGHT 53 left the ship as a hull painted into the map. The owner had said what it was always meant to be: the ship is an entity, the land is an entity, and the map is only a blank parallax backdrop. Two days went into that, before anyone died.

**MAXINE:** What is a vehicle entity, in the engine?

**TOMO:** A piece with its own folder. A state file with a position, a size, which desk it shows, and whether it lights itself. A model file with a small grid of voxels. The board engine draws it, so moving the ship means changing two numbers, not rewriting the book and reloading for five seconds.

**RAHWEH:** Direct flight is the second helm mode. Arrow keys move the ship entity. The rule the owner set was strict: when you pilot, the picture must not change. The ship stays fixed on the screen, the land scrolls under it, the camera follows.

**MAXINE:** And up and down?

**TOMO:** X and Z. Level keys read from the selector, the helm lifts by one, the ship and the land pieces move opposite, so the picture stays. Measured: x three times gave one, two, three. Two frames showed the ship over the planet at different heights. Not done: collision, and one z press was dropped by the test harness.

**THE NARRATOR:** Then the planet. Once the land was its own entity it looked flat next to the old rpg-dust worlds, which were textured and extruded. The owner asked why not paint it with the old look and still keep it an entity. So the engine overlays the planet page onto the empty cells of the shown desk, driven by one small land file with a position, an altitude and a source. And the missing centre came back the same way as before: a second solid run under the hull.

**RAHWEH:** One more request: a debug frame. A grey wireframe cube for the bounds in three dimensions, a grey ring in two, toggled by a line in view.pdl. The first cube was green, and invisible because a flag was reset every frame. The owner had to say, more than once, I do not see it. Fixed by computing the box before the early return.

**MAXINE:** Is it all verified?

**TOMO:** Honestly, no. The overlay logs ran, the cube was seen in the live window top down, and the planet body showed under the hull. The two dimensional ring, a clean angled frame of cube and planet, altitude with the overlay, and the release were not re-run.

**THE NARRATOR:** That was the ship. Then, late on the second day, the direction changed.

**THE NARRATOR:** Then, in the evening, the ship was set down. By evening the owner said: never mind all that, we are moving in a new direction. Deaths and respawns.

**MAXINE:** Who dies?

**TOMO:** Everyone, pets included. Hunger and damage now. Vacuum, ozone and spacesuits later, all as events. A dead character respawns on the ship bridge, one safe place, and half of what they carry falls where they died. Anyone can pick it up.

**RAHWEH:** And the numbers live in a plain file, life.pdl. The drop share, permadeath or respawn, the respawn spot. Edit it and the daemon notices within two seconds.

**MAXINE:** How does a game like RPG Maker do it?

**TOMO:** Death is just a state, and the game over or the respawn is an event, a common event. So we copied the shape: hit points reach zero, the Death state is set, and a page called death plays.

**THE NARRATOR:** Two pages, death and respawn, each with one node that calls a verb. The death page drops the items. In respawn mode it plays the respawn page, which sends the character back to the bridge with full health. If a page is missing, the same effect runs directly.

**RAHWEH:** Proof was the ledgers. A killing hit, and both pages logged: Play event page one, trigger parallel, in the same second. Then the DEATH row, with three of six bread dropped, and the RESPAWN row at the bridge.

**MAXINE:** And the health?

**TOMO:** A small bar after each pet's name in the bottom bar. Green when well, red and short under thirty percent, grey when knocked out.

**RAHWEH:** That took longer than the death. The first try put the bars in the empty space of the footer. The owner wanted them inside each pet's own slot. The second try used a border colour, and the renderer ignores it. The third used a line break in the label, and the whole slot vanished.

**THE NARRATOR:** The renderer drops any item whose label has a line break. So the bar sits after the name, and the slot is narrow enough that it wraps by itself. A true second line needs the shared renderer, which nobody touched.

**MAXINE:** Why did the bar lose some pets?

**TOMO:** Two reasons. It lists only pets in the current room, which is old behaviour the owner accepted. And a test with permadeath had left one pet dead. A reset brought everyone back.

**RAHWEH:** Small traps worth writing down. The projector does not restart itself. The style file is read only when the window opens. And a screenshot fails with write failed when its folder is gone.

**THE NARRATOR:** Not done: drops are not drawn in the scene, nothing yet deals damage except the hurt verb, and the second line of the bar waits for the renderer.

**MAXINE:** So what is the rule for tomorrow?

**TOMO:** A character that dies is not an error. It is a state, with a page that handles it. Build the next cause of death as another event, not another branch of code.
