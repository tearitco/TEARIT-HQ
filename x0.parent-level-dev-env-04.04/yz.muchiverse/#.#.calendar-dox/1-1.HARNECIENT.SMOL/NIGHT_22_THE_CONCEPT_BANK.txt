**MAXINE:** So the discovery isn't "training is broken." Training works. The discovery is a different, harder question.

**TOMO:** Nobody can look at row three, column forty-seven, and know what editing it does. That's not a bug in those specific numbers. That's what a dense matrix is. Gradient descent doesn't fail to be legible — it succeeds at being illegible, on purpose, because it packs multiple unrelated concepts into the same weight to save capacity. Superposition. **RAHWEH:** And here's the part that stopped being a live debate fast, because it was already answered — just not where anyone had looked recently. `#.Z.HUMAN_LLM/^.2DO.aug01_2026.txt`. Tomom's own to-do doc. Already says it, in these words: "hand-tunable, modular, auditable LLM track."

**MAXINE:** It forecloses GPU-brute-force as the goal. Nobody signed up to out-scale a real transformer. The stated goal was always precise, hand-and-fine-tuned adjustment, using meta-harness techniques the house already has. Dense matrices don't fail that goal by accident. **TOMO:** Then say what already exists in this house that WOULD satisfy that goal, because that's the turn that actually opened tonight's design.

**RAHWEH:** The Bank Layer. Synonym Bank, Relation Bank, Behavior Bank, Sentence Bank. Sparse. Named. Keyed. Laplace-smoothed. Auditable by construction — every entry is a real row you can read and explain. The house solved "hand-tunable, legible learning" once already. Tomom just never got handed that same discipline.

**MAXINE:** So the proposal, first pass, plainly.

**TOMO:** A new Bank. Concept Bank. Named abstract z-nodes — `force`, `motion`, `energy`, `quantity`, `structure`, `change` — and words point into it with weighted membership instead of getting baked into opaque matrix rows.

**RAHWEH:** Wait. That's not quite right yet. Say the problem with "words point into concepts" as a flat design before it gets built on.

**MAXINE:** Go on.

**RAHWEH:** If it's pure pairwise relations — this word relates to this concept, this concept relates to that concept — you eventually need a direct authored relation between every pair that matters. That doesn't scale, and it's not actually what makes generalization work. What you need is a shared, lower-dimensional layer that lots of words across lots of different corpora can all point into at once, so two words that never appear near each other can still turn out related, through what they both point at.

**TOMO:** Say what that actually is, underneath.

**RAHWEH:** It's an embedding dimension. It's a hidden layer. That's the actual mathematical trick a normal neural net already does — shared compositional structure, one small set of latent directions that many surface features route through. We're not inventing a new trick. We're taking a trick that already works and doing it explicitly, named, and by hand, instead of implicitly and by gradient descent.

**MAXINE:** Good — that's worth landing on. Same math, opposite legibility. Now, the record itself. What is a z-node actually made of, on disk.

**TOMO:** Second correction, and this one's specific. A z-node isn't a flat label with a weight hanging off it. It's a record with a fixed number of pointer-slots. Tunable constant — start at four to eight slots, leave room to grow to thirty-two or more. Each slot is a pair: pointer, weight. Empty and zero by default until something actually fills it.

**RAHWEH:** Say why fixed-width with mostly-empty slots is better than just an open list that grows.

**TOMO:** Because a fixed shape gives you real operations for free. Trim — a slot's weight decays toward the floor, you zero it, that's pruning, same discipline the Bank Layer already runs on decaying entries. Beef up — a new relation gets discovered or authored, you fill the next empty slot. And compression — on disk you only ever write the non-zero slots, sparse form, same principle the vocab table already uses for its bias fields. At load time you expand back to the full fixed width in memory. Cheap on disk, uniform in memory.

**MAXINE:** Now the third correction. This is the one that actually decides whether this whole thing stays auditable.

**RAHWEH:** Right — and it's the one I'd call most important. First draft of this design let pointers go anywhere. Word to word, concept to concept, word to concept, freely. Say why that's wrong before saying what replaces it.

**TOMO:** Because "freely" is a maze. If any node can point at any other node, auditing one relation means you can't bound how far the chain runs before you've checked it. You lose the property that made the Bank Layer trustworthy in the first place.

**RAHWEH:** So: hub-and-spoke, not a web. Every pointer resolves to a MASTER word or concept only. Never directly to another spoke. That's not a new idea — it's WordNet synsets, the real, decades-proven pattern. Every word sense points to exactly one canonical synset. Never straight to another word sense. Bounded fan-out. No chains-of-chains to untangle when you're trying to explain why a weight is what it is.

**MAXINE:** Does that mean concepts can't relate to other concepts? `force` clearly relates to `motion`.

**RAHWEH:** They can — `force` pointing at `motion` at weight 0.7, at `energy` at 0.5, same slot-record shape a word uses to point at its master. One generic slotted-pointer type, reused at the word level and the concept level. Not two formats. The house already prefers few, flexible, growable primitives over one-off shapes — this just applies that same taste here.

**THE NARRATOR:** There's a normalization question sitting under all of this. Say it, and say how it got resolved.

**TOMO:** If a spoke points at a master with a weight, does the master need to know that too? Where does the weight actually live — at the spoke, at the master, both?

**MAXINE:** Walk the resolution, because it wasn't guessed, it was reasoned through.

**RAHWEH:** Weights live in exactly one place — the spoke's own forward record. A word's outbound slots, pointing at the masters it relates to, and their weights. That's the single authoritative fact. That's the thing a human, or Gemma, or tomom itself, actually hand-edits.

**TOMO:** And the master's side?

**RAHWEH:** The master holds a mirror table — everything currently pointing at it, and their weights — for fast reverse lookup. But that mirror is derived. Regenerated. Never a second authoritative copy you could edit independently and have it drift from the spoke's own record.

**MAXINE:** Tie that to something concrete, because it's not an abstract worry.

**RAHWEH:** It's the exact bug class this same session already hit earlier tonight, on a completely different system — the phymoji sprite cache going stale because nothing regenerated it when the source sprite actually changed. Same failure mode. A cached mirror, hand-editable in its own right, quietly disagreeing with its own source. The fix is the same fix both times: one real source of truth, everything else rebuildable from it, never hand-patched in two places at once.

**TOMO:** So say the practical payoff of choosing it that way.

**RAHWEH:** O(1) lookup in both directions — a word's own relations, or everything pointing at a given master — with zero risk of the two copies disagreeing, because there's only ever one copy that was ever authoritative.

**THE NARRATOR:** One more axis got raised and it needs to be named honestly as unresolved, not folded into the rest as if it were settled.

**MAXINE:** RL and IRL.

**RAHWEH:** GOAP.

**MAXINE:** Action preconditions and effects can carry concept pointers too — increases `force`, decreases `disorder`. That lets a planner reason abstractly, closer to how a person actually describes what they want, instead of only matching literal symbolic preconditions.

**THE NARRATOR:** Say the actual payoff, plainly, once all four of those point through the same substrate.

**TOMO:** Bank entries, FSM transitions, GOAP preconditions, tomom's own concept membership — once they all point through the same named substrate, one piece of feedback propagates across all of them at once instead of needing a separate tuning pass for each. **RAHWEH:** And that only works if feedback itself carries enough shape to route. A bare up-or-down number can't say which concept it's about.
