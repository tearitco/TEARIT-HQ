# The Living Testament

## Book of the Soul Pen

### Chapter 8

### The Kingdom Beneath the Red Sky

**1** And it came to pass during the Days of Separation, after Sol had vanished from among them and no message could be received from him, that Lucky, Astra, Mara, and the Free Colonists emerged from the forgotten tunnels and entered once again into the upper districts of New Antioch, desiring both refuge and knowledge, for they understood that ignorance had become more dangerous than any enemy.

**2** Now the city appeared fair unto the eye, and its towers reflected the light of the artificial suns, and its marketplaces were filled with merchants from many worlds; yet beneath the appearance of prosperity there lingered a feeling difficult to describe, as though the entire colony breathed according to a rhythm not wholly its own.

**3** Therefore Lucky walked among the crowds observing the people, and he perceived that many carried small crystal containers upon their belts, and others drank openly from vessels containing crimson liquid, and still others exchanged packets of the same substance as payment for goods and services.

**4** Then he inquired of Mara, saying, "What manner of drink is this, which all men seem to value above water itself?"

**5** And Mara answered, "Type-C blood; for upon Mars the atmosphere is hostile, and though the domes preserve life, many workers spend long hours beyond them, therefore modified blood hath become both medicine and necessity, and over many years necessity became commerce."

**6** Then Astra said, "So blood became money."

**7** And Mara replied, "Money, fuel, medicine, privilege, and power, for there are districts where a wealthy man may purchase enough blood to prolong his strength for years, while the poor sell their own supply merely to survive another week."

**8** And hearing these things Lucky was troubled, for he remembered the Blood Banks beneath the city and began to perceive that what appeared to be an economy was in truth a vast circulatory system extending throughout the colony.

**9** As they journeyed farther, they came unto industrial districts where great machines operated day and night, and there they saw clone laborers unloading cargo, maintaining reactors, and repairing transit systems, while overseers monitored their efficiency from elevated platforms.

**10** Now the clones resembled ordinary men and women in appearance, and many spoke, laughed, and conversed among themselves; yet there remained something sorrowful in their eyes, as though they possessed memories that were incomplete.

**11** Then Astra observed a young clone carrying a heavy container and asked him, saying, "How long hast thou worked here?"

**12** But the clone smiled strangely and answered, "I was printed three months ago, and I shall likely be recycled within another three."

**13** And having spoken these words, he continued his labor as though he had merely discussed the weather.

**14** Then Astra stood silent.

**15** For she knew not how to answer such a thing.

**16** And Lucky likewise was disturbed, for though he had witnessed many strange technologies since leaving Earth, never before had he encountered a society where men spoke so casually concerning their own replacement.

**17** Thus they continued through the city until they reached a district known as the Arena of Iron, and there they found vast crowds gathered before enormous gates adorned with banners, symbols, and holographic displays visible from many streets away.

**18** Then Mara said unto them, "If ye desire to understand Mars, ye must understand what the people celebrate."

**19** Therefore they entered.

**20** And the arena was immense, being capable of holding tens of thousands, and every seat was occupied by spectators shouting, wagering, laughing, and arguing concerning the contests below.

**21** Then trumpets sounded, and mechanical voices proclaimed the beginning of the Games, and upon the field there entered two teams, one composed of engineered athletes from Earth and another composed entirely of combat clones bred for strength and endurance.

**22** Now the ball used in the contest was larger than any ordinary sphere, requiring several players to maneuver it effectively, and the rules thereof appeared to have evolved from basketball into something far stranger.

**23** For players leapt great distances, activated exo-suits, utilized magnetic fields, and launched themselves through the air while the crowd roared approval, and whenever a goal was scored fireworks erupted from the walls and great quantities of Type-C blood were awarded unto victorious supporters.

**24** Then Astra laughed despite herself and said, "This may be the most ridiculous thing I have witnessed since arriving upon Mars."

**25** Yet Mara answered, "Wait."

**26** And after the first contest concluded, the second began.

**27** Then there entered machines.

**28** Great metallic athletes equipped with launchers, stabilizers, and rotating limbs, and they competed against enhanced clone teams while engineers monitored their performance and gamblers exchanged fortunes in the stands.

**29** And many among the crowd preferred the machines.

**30** For machines rarely disappointed expectations.

**31** Yet all these things served merely as entertainment before the final spectacle.

**32** Then the lights dimmed.

**33** And the arena floor opened.

**34** And from beneath arose a gigantic construct fashioned of steel, ceramics, and living tissue intertwined, whose arms ended in cannons capable of launching spheres the size of vehicles.

**35** The crowd immediately rose to its feet, and many began chanting ancient slogans known throughout the colony, while children climbed upon their seats to behold the machine more clearly.

**36** Therefore Lucky asked Mara, "What is this thing?"

**37** And she answered, "The Champion Engine."

**38** "Some call it the King of Courts."

**39** "Others call it the Ball-God."

**40** "Most simply call it unbeatable."

**41** Then the machine commenced its contest, and each impact shook portions of the arena, while clone athletes scattered before its attacks and attempted desperately to outmaneuver it.

**42** Yet despite its size it moved with astonishing precision, and before long victory belonged unto the construct, causing great celebration throughout the stadium.

**43** Thereafter a procession began.

**44** Music sounded.

**45** Banners descended.

**46** Nobles entered their reserved chambers.

**47** And military officers assembled near the central platform.

**48** Then the crowds became noticeably quieter.

**49** For someone of great importance had arrived.

**50** And all eyes turned toward the royal balcony.

**51** There appeared the Black Queen.

**52** Now she was taller than most women, and her dark exo-armor was adorned with symbols of authority, conquest, and industry, while mechanical attendants accompanied her on either side.

**53** Yet what struck Lucky most was not her appearance but the reaction of the people.

**54** For none cheered as one cheereth a beloved ruler.

**55** Neither did they tremble as before a tyrant.

**56** Rather they regarded her as sailors regard a storm at sea: dangerous, necessary, and beyond their ability to command.

**57** Then the Queen surveyed the arena.

**58** And though she spoke only briefly, every voice fell silent to hear her words.

**59** Saying, "Let industry prosper."

**60** "Let order prevail."

**61** "Let Mars endure."

**62** And the multitude answered as one.

**63** "Let Mars endure."

**64** Then Astra whispered, "She ruleth differently than I expected."

**65** And Mara replied, "Most monsters do."

**66** Yet before further discussion could be had, another spectacle commenced.

**67** For prisoners were brought forth.

**68** Some wore painted smiles.

**69** Some laughed despite their chains.

**70** Some sang songs which caused discomfort among the listeners.

**71** Then Lucky observed that many spectators became uneasy at their appearance.

**72** For these were clowns.

**73** And even among those who hated the Queen, few desired the company of clowns.

**74** Then the prisoners were placed upon a great mechanical transport shaped like a winding railway, and the crowd watched in solemn silence as the machine activated.

**75** Therefore Lucky asked, "What judgment is this?"

**76** And Mara answered grimly, "The Long-Winded Goodbye."

**77** Then the machine carried the prisoners through a labyrinth of spinning gears, flame projectors, crushing mechanisms, and automated sermons proclaiming the virtues of civic obedience.

**78** And some among the prisoners laughed throughout the ordeal.

**79** While others screamed.

**80** Yet one clown stood silently until the machine reached its highest point.

**81** Then suddenly he broke his silence and cried out with a voice so loud that even the farthest seats heard him.

**82** Saying, "Fools! Ye know not what approacheth!"

**83** And many mocked him.

**84** Yet he continued.

**85** "The Queen feareth what she created!"

**86** Then a strange thing occurred.

**87** For the laughter ceased.

**88** The crowd became silent.

**89** The guards exchanged glances.

**90** Even Mara lowered her eyes.

**91** And Astra immediately perceived that the words possessed meaning.

**92** Then the clown stretched forth his shackled hands and shouted again.

**93** "Ask her of the First Nest!"

**94** "Ask her of the Mothers!"

**95** "Ask her what sleepeth beneath the blood!"

**96** Thereupon the execution machine completed its course.

**97** And the clown vanished into fire.

**98** Yet his words remained.

**99** Then Lucky looked toward the royal balcony.

**100** And for the briefest moment he saw something unexpected upon the face of the Black Queen.

**101** Not anger.

**102** Not pride.

**103** Not contempt.

**104** But fear.

**105** The moment passed quickly.

**106** Yet Lucky knew what he had seen.

**107** And Astra knew it also.

**108** Then the Queen rose and departed with her attendants, and the crowds slowly returned to their conversations as though nothing unusual had occurred.

**109** Yet beneath the surface of those conversations a question had taken root, and once planted it refused to die.

**110** For if the Queen truly feared something, then what power existed upon Mars greater than the ruler of Mars herself?

**111** Thus ended the day of spectacles.

**112** And thus began the search for the First Nest.

**113** For the shadow of Carcazod stretched farther than they had imagined, and the secrets buried beneath New Antioch were older, deeper, and more terrible than any of them yet understood.
